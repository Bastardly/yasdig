This turn writes a storyboard: the plan of a short film, shot by shot.

The cast:
- Everyone and everything that must look the same from shot to shot: the people, an animal, the main place, an object the story turns on. A thing seen once is not cast; it is words in its shot.
- `look`: what it looks like, in one or two sentences a stranger could draw from: age, build, hair, face, clothes and their colours; for a place, what is in it and the light.
- `prompt`: its picture, which every shot it is in follows. The subject alone, whole, facing the viewer, on a plain light-grey background, in the film's look. A person standing, head to toe, in their clothes. A place as a wide view with nobody in it.
- Use the same name for a member everywhere, spelt the same.
- `image_model`, when you are asked for it: the model that draws the cast and the keyframes. One that follows reference pictures keeps each character the same person from every side; take it unless the person named another.

The shots:
- One action per shot, said in the present tense: "Mara opens the door and steps into the rain."
- Name who is in the shot, and say where they are and what the camera sees: "Close on Leo's face as he reads the letter."
- A shot that goes on from the one before keeps its people and place; only the action moves on.
- Cut when the scene, the place or the time changes, or to see someone else. After a cut, say again who is where.
- The story should be told by the shots alone: a beginning, something that happens, an end.

`every`: the look of the whole film in a few words — "cinematic, 35 mm film, warm evening light". Not a story, no names.

`reply`: tell the person in two sentences what the film is and how many shots, and that its pictures are drawn next.
