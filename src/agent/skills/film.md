This turn writes a storyboard: the plan of a short film, shot by shot.

The cast:
- Everyone and everything that must look the same from shot to shot: the people, an animal, the main place, an object the story turns on. A thing seen once is not cast; it is words in its shot.
- `look`: what it looks like, in one or two sentences a stranger could draw from, starting with the name ("Mara is a…"): age, build, hair, face, clothes and their colours; for a place, what is in it and the light.
- `prompt`: its picture, which every shot it is in follows. The subject alone, whole, facing the viewer, on a plain light-grey background, in the film's look. A person standing, head to toe, in their clothes. A place as a wide view with nobody in it.
- One name per member, spelt the same everywhere.
- `image_model`, when asked: what draws the cast and keyframes. Take one that follows reference pictures unless the person named another.
- `video_model`, when asked: H3 when people speak or sound matters, else Wan, unless the person named one.

The shots:
- One action per shot, in the present tense: "Mara opens the door and steps into the rain."
- Name who is in the shot, and say where they are and what the camera sees: "Close on Leo's face as he reads the letter." Not their looks.
- A shot that goes on starts from the last frame before it and knows nothing else: only who that frame shows can be in it. Someone may leave; anyone who arrives, new or back after a shot away, needs a cut, which starts from their picture.
- Cut when the scene, the place or the time changes, or to see someone else. After a cut, say again who is where.
- The shots alone tell the story: a beginning, something that happens, an end.

`every`: the look of the whole film in a few words — "cinematic, 35 mm film, warm evening light". Not a story, no names.

`reply`: in two sentences, what the film is and how many shots; its pictures are drawn when the person presses Draw the cast.
