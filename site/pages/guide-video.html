Video

A video model of this size runs out of memory a few seconds in, so the Video tab builds a video from pieces, on the Storyboard: a video holds scenes, a scene holds shots, and a shot holds parts. This page is how they fit together; the Video page lists the models.

Video › Scene › Shot › Part

  • A video has a title and a Generation button under it: the model and its files, LoRAs, size, frame rate, Hi-res, steps, CFG, sampler and the Advanced fold. Every shot is made with these, so the shots join.
  • A scene is one place and time. Click its name for the Scene panel: a short header, the Scene description — who and where, and the look, put in front of every part of its shots (its {variables} roll once for the scene, so a rolled character stays the same) — and the Negative prompt.
  • A shot is one camera run. Between two shots is a cut: the next one starts fresh. + Shot adds one, ↑↓ move it (across scenes too), × deletes it. Click a shot to put it on the stage and open the Shot panel.
  • A part is one run of the model: its own seed, frames and prompt, side by side in the Shot panel. Each part after the first goes on from the last frame of the one before. A part is conditioned on its own words after the scene description, so a line spoken in part 1 is spoken once — say only what happens in it. Part 1's Start from image is the shot's first frame; without one the model runs as plain text-to-video.

Only one frame carries across a part's seam, so a face that leaves the picture and comes back is drawn anew: write parts that agree, and change camera, place or who is in view at a cut. Wan TI2V-5B and A14B, MiniMax H3 and LTX go on from a last frame; other models make one part per shot.

Generating: cheap first, then good

Generate video on the rail draws every shot that isn't done, in storyboard order, one at a time; Generate scene does a scene, Generate shot the shot on the stage. Stop ends the run; the shots already made stay.

After a render every part shows the seed it was drawn with. Change one part's seed, length or words and the button becomes Redo from part k: the parts before it are kept, and that one and the ones after it are drawn again from the last frame kept. Add a part and it draws only the new one onto the end. So draft at a few steps, fix each shot until it works, then raise the steps and generate again — the same seeds, drawn properly. A shot drawn again keeps its old frames until the new ones are done.

The video's settings ask first

Once a shot is generated, the model, size, frame rate and Hi-res belong to the whole video: shots made differently can't be joined. Changing one asks first, since the shots already generated would need generating again — say yes and they are marked outdated, cancel and the value goes back. A generated scene's description and negative ask the same way. Steps, CFG, LoRAs and memory are free to change. Flow shift for this shot, under its parts, overrides the video's for one shot.

First frames, references and sounds

Any shot's part 1 can start from an image — a keyframe drawn on the Image tab, the way a Wan video changes scene. With MiniMax H3 a shot that starts fresh may instead follow up to four reference pictures (a face, a costume, a place) and up to three sounds (a voice to speak in, 15 seconds in all), and its parts have Who is in it, Spoken (Mara: Who's there?), Sound and Music, which YASDIG writes into MiniMax's own prompt layout. Not a first frame and references on one shot: sd.cpp can't give H3 both.

Enhance

Enhance under a part's prompt has the Assistant's model write it out in full for the video's model: on LTX one paragraph with the action, the camera and every sound, since LTX makes the soundtrack from the same words; on Wan picture and motion only; on H3 what is seen, with Spoken and Sound filled when empty. It is shown the scene's first part, the part before and how the next shot begins, so the place, light and people carry on — and a shot you put first leads into the ones after it. It adds no one your words don't put there. Enhance every part does a shot's parts in order. Ctrl+Z puts your words back.

Films with the Assistant

Ask the Video tab's Assistant for a short film and it writes it straight into a storyboard of its own, with the cast in the Cast panel. To work on a video you made yourself, open it and start a new chat beside it: the chat takes the open video as its film on your first message.

  • It sees the storyboard as you left it on every turn, and names shots the way the storyboard numbers them — say “shot 2” as you see it.
  • Parts you wrote or changed are yours: it changes them only when you ask, and a shot of yours it leaves out is kept. Only a shot it wrote, untouched and not generated, is removed when it leaves it out, and the chat says which.
  • A generated shot it rewrites keeps its frames and shows as changed until you generate it again. The settings are the video's own.

Playing, saving and sharing

The stage plays the whole video, shot after shot, each with its own sound; the strip under the scrubber jumps to a shot. Click the picture for the video dialog: Enlarge runs FSR over every frame, or with LTX installed redraws each shot at twice its size without flicker; Save to output writes the video as one file, every shot joined; Download makes an MP4, WebM or GIF in your browser. A video saves itself once it is generated or saved; star it to keep it past Delete all.

When LTX-2.5 ignores the prompt

On some pairs of seed and length, LTX-2.5 distilled draws something unrelated to the words — a man talking to camera, someone chopping vegetables — while the same words at another seed or length come out right. It is a known fault in stable-diffusion.cpp (#2220), not in your prompt: give the shot a new seed, or change its length by one step, and generate it again.
